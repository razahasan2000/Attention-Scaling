\section*{S1. Full architecture specification}\label{sup:arch}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{S1. Full architecture specification}

\subsection*{S1.1 Layer inventory}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Token embeddings}: \texttt{nn.Embedding(18855, 128, padding\_idx=0)}.
  \item \textbf{Positional embeddings}: learned \texttt{nn.Parameter} of shape
        $(1, 128, 128)$, initialised as $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.02^2)$.
  \item \textbf{Encoder blocks} ($\times 2$): pre-LN Transformer block ---
        $\operatorname{LayerNorm} \to$ multi-head self-attention $\to$ residual
        add, then $\operatorname{LayerNorm} \to$ feed-forward (GELU) $\to$
        residual add. Query, key, value, and output projections are bias-free
        \texttt{nn.Linear(128, 128)}; the feed-forward is
        $128 \to 256 \to 128$ with GELU.
  \item \textbf{Final LayerNorm}: \texttt{nn.LayerNorm(128)}.
  \item \textbf{Classification head}: \texttt{nn.Linear(128, num\_classes)}.
  \item \textbf{Language-modelling head}: \texttt{nn.Linear(128, 18855,
        bias=False)} with weight tying to the token embedding.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{S1.3 Scaling variants}
For $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times \dk}$ and effective divisor $s$:
\begin{align*}
  \text{Standard:}\quad & \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(QK^{\top}/\sqrt{\dk}\right)V,
  & s &= \sqrt{32} \approx 5.6569, \\
  \text{Temperature:}\quad & \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(QK^{\top}/(\tau\sqrt{\dk})\right)V,
  & s &= \tau\sqrt{32} \approx 11.3137 \;\; (\tau = 2), \\
  \text{Alternative:}\quad & \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(QK^{\top}/\dk\right)V,
  & s &= 32.
\end{align*}
The mixed-scale ablation arm (\texttt{multiscale\_v1}) divides the logits of
heads 1--2 by $\sqrt{\dk}$ (standard) and heads 3--4 by $\dk$; the
learning-rate compensation arm (\texttt{dk\_lrcomp}) multiplies the learning
rates of $W_q$ and $W_k$ in the $1/\dk$ variant by $\sqrt{\dk} \approx 5.66$.

\subsection*{S1.4 Unit tests}
Five test suites (22 tests) cover: bit-identical initialization across variants
for a fixed seed; gradient flow through every variant; output shapes and
dtype contracts; masked-attention correctness (padding positions receive
negligible weight); and data-pipeline determinism (identical batch order for
identical seeds). Run with \texttt{pytest tests/}.

\subsection*{S2.2 Splits and tokenization}
Classification training sets are subsampled to 10{,}000 examples with a 20\%
held-out validation split (SST-2 uses the official GLUE validation/test splits:
1{,}115 / 1{,}821); test sets are the frozen official test partitions. Text is
tokenised with the regular expression \texttt{[a-z0-9']+} over lowercased input
(classification) or whitespace splitting (LM, with an \texttt{<eos>} token).
Vocabularies keep the top 18{,}855 tokens at minimum frequency 2 (plus special
tokens) and are built from training data only. Label mappings preserve the
original class identities (AG News 1--4 $\to$ 0--3; IMDB neg/pos $\to$ 0/1).
Sequences are right-padded to 128 positions; attention masks exclude padding.

\subsection*{S6.2 Effect-size interpretation}
Following Cohen \cite{cohen1988}: $|d_z| < 0.2$ negligible; $0.2$--$0.5$ small;
$0.5$--$0.8$ medium; $> 0.8$ large. Only the two AG News comparisons exceed the
negligible band ($d_z = 1.52$ large; $d_z = 0.53$ medium).

\section*{S17. The divisor power law: derivation and validation}\label{sup:law}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{S17. The divisor power law: derivation and validation}

\subsection*{S17.1 Derivation}
Consider one attention row with pre-softmax logits $X_1, \dots, X_n$ treated
as i.i.d.\ with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$ (the raw, pre-division scale),
and two divisors $c_1 > c_2$ with inverse scales $b_i = 1/c_i$. Writing
$P_b(j) = e^{b X_j}/\sum_k e^{b X_k}$ and expanding $P_b$ around $b = 0$,

\begin{align}
  P_b(j) &= \frac{1}{n}\Bigl[1 + b\,(X_j - \bar X)
           + \tfrac{b^2}{2}\Bigl(X_j^2 - \overline{X^2}\Bigr)
           - b^2 \bar X (X_j - \bar X)\Bigr] + O(b^3),
  \qquad \bar X = \tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_k X_k,
\end{align}
so to second order $P_{b_1}(j) - P_{b_2}(j)
= (b_1 - b_2)\,\tfrac{1}{n}(X_j - \bar X) + O(b^3)$. Substituting into
$\mathrm{KL}(P_{b_1}\|P_{b_2}) = \sum_j P_{b_1}(j)\log\frac{P_{b_1}(j)}{P_{b_2}(j)}$
and taking expectations over the logit distribution,

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{KL}(P_{b_1}\|P_{b_2})
  \;\approx\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,(b_1 - b_2)^2\,\sigma^2\,\Bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{n}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:sup-law}
\end{equation}
where the $(1 - 1/n)$ factor is the finite-population correction (with the
empirical variance $\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_j (X_j - \bar X)^2$ in place of
$\sigma^2$, the leading term is exactly
$\tfrac{1}{2}(b_1-b_2)^2\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(X)\bigr]$).
Equation~\eqref{eq:sup-law} is Eq.~(5) of the main text. Its validity range
is $b\,\sigma \lesssim 0.5$ per unit $\Delta b$; beyond that, softmax
saturation (weights piling onto the maximum logit) makes the true divergence
grow sub-quadratically, which the Monte Carlo grid below exhibits at
$\sigma \ge 12$.

\subsection*{S18.4 Frontier probe: per-layer diagnostics}
The pretrained-decoder probe (Table 12 of the main text) records, per layer,
the raw pre-division logit SD over causally valid entries, the per-row
attention entropy under each divisor, and the per-row KL divergence between
the attention maps the same logits induce under the standard and alternative
divisors (softmax restricted to the causal horizon). GPT-2 (124M): layer
1--6 entropies of 2.45, 2.93, 2.15, 1.67, 1.62, 1.38 nats under the training
divisor, rising to 3.81, 3.86, 3.72, 3.65, 3.61, 3.80 under $1/\dk$; the
mean standard-vs-$1/\dk$ divergence is 1.42 nats per row (quadratic law
prediction 31.5 nats, the well-known saturation gap at $b\sigma \gg 1$).
GPT-2-medium (355M): mean $\sigma_{\text{raw}} = 44.8$, mean divergence
1.51 nats. Absolute perplexity in this probe depends on the windowing
protocol (non-overlapping 128-token windows, no cache carry-over); the
\emph{contrasts} between divisors do not.
